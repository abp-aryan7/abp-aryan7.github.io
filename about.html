About Me

Electrical and Mechanical Engineering student at Georgia Tech, lifelong learner, builder, and someone who has never quite stopped asking why.

Throughout high school, I was repeatedly told to become a "lifelong learner in the pursuit of excellence." While at the time, I took it to be a simple motto, I have now realized that my mind has naturally gravitated towards its meaning.

I've always believed that knowledge has its greatest value when it can be used to improve the lives of others. Whether it's developing more sustainable technologies, creating more efficient engineering solutions, or simply helping someone better understand a difficult concept, I want the work I do to leave the world a little better than I found it. That desire has become the driving force behind everything I choose to learn. To make a meaningful impact, however, I first have to understand the problems I'm trying to solve.

I have always found myself asking why. Instead of accepting that some things just happen, I have always found myself wanting to understand the fundamental principles behind them. Whether I am building circuits, coding simulations, studying financial markets, or learning new languages, I'm constantly looking for the reasoning that ties everything together. I believe that a deeper understanding not only makes solving problems easier, but also opens the door to asking better questions.

"What I cannot create, I do not understand." Richard Feynman

As I continued to explore engineering and science, I discovered the work and philosophy of physicist Richard Feynman. This perspective reinforced a belief that I had already started leaning towards: the best way to truly learn is to build, experiment, and explain ideas from their first principles. It has shaped how I approach every single project, from constructing my first 8-bit breadboard computer to researching different stocks.

While engineering has taught me how to solve technical problems, serving as a youth leader at my local Hindu temple has taught me how to connect with people. Planning activities, mentoring younger students, and explaining ideas in an engaging way has shown me that understanding isn't complete until it can be shared. Those experiences have strengthened my communication, leadership, and patience just as much as my engineering projects have strengthened my technical skills.

Today, as an Electrical and Mechanical Engineering student at the Georgia Institute of Technology, I continue to pursue opportunities that challenge me to think deeply, build thoughtfully, and serve others. Whether I'm exploring sustainable energy technologies, designing new engineering solutions, mentoring younger students, or learning something entirely outside my field, my goal remains the same: to continuously learn, to create with purpose, and to use that knowledge to make a meaningful and lasting contribution to the world.
